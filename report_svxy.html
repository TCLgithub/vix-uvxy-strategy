VIX above 25: does buying the mean-reversion actually work?

Backtest and Monte Carlo verification of the mirror-image idea to the UVXY momentum test: when VIX spikes to an elevated level, it tends to revert back down — the "volatility risk premium." SVXY (short VIX futures) profits from that reversion. Tested on SVXY, 2011–2026.

Meaningfully more credible than the UVXY momentum idea — balanced win/loss sizing, a real economic rationale, and entries that structurally land after the worst single-day gap risk, not before it. But the stop-loss has only been tested 4 times in 15 years, and part of this return is "short vol was a winning trade in this era generally," not purely signal edge. See caveats below before sizing this with real capital.

02What was tested

  • Signal: VIX daily close above 25.00 (elevated/fear zone)
  • Entry: next day's open in SVXY (no lookahead)
  • Exit, first to trigger: +10% take profit · −20% stop loss · 20 trading days max hold
  • Sizing: one position at a time, full capital redeployed per trade (a stress test, not a sizing recommendation)
  • Instrument: SVXY (ProShares Short VIX Short-Term Futures ETF), 2011-10-04 inception through present
Leverage regime change: SVXY ran at −1.0× VIX-futures exposure until Feb 2018, when ProShares cut it to −0.5× in response to "Volmageddon" (the event that killed the XIV ETN outright in a single session). 17 of the 54 trades in this backtest predate that change, 37 postdate it — the two halves aren't fully apples-to-apples, though the strategy held up across both.

04Stress-tested against the two real crises in this window

Unlike a passive short-vol position (which was already exposed going into a spike), this signal only enters after VIX has already closed elevated — so by construction it tends to land after the worst single-day move, not before it. Checked against the two genuine crises SVXY has lived through:

SVXY itself lost roughly −80% overnight on Feb 5–6, 2018 — the event that killed the XIV ETN outright. This backtest's Feb 5 signal only triggers an entry on Feb 6, after that reset, at the already-crashed price — it was never exposed to the actual blowup. And when VIX kept spiking for days in a row (March 9, 2020), the −20% stop did what it was supposed to do. The strategy also happened to buy within days of the literal COVID peak (VIX 82.7, March 16) and caught the reversion from there.

Read the tail with caution: outcomes above ~1000% come from compounding many favorable trades back-to-back in a resampled order — a mathematical property of compounding 54 trades' worth of returns, not a number to expect on average. The median (+925.9%) and the spread around it are the more honest read than the eye-catching 95th-percentile figure.

Random-entry control (5,000 draws): same exit rule, entries chosen at random instead of gated on VIX>25.

VIX>25 signal (bootstrap of real trades) Random-entry control
Read this one carefully. The real strategy beats the random control 100% of the time on total return — but only 8.8% of the time on win rate (control's mean win rate is actually higher, 83.1% vs 77.8%). The random control also made money on average (median +11.5%). That means part of this edge is simply "being short vol in general was a winning trade in this era" (SVXY's structural decay tailwind) — the VIX>25 timing amplifies the size of the wins substantially, but isn't the sole source of profitability. Don't attribute the full 889% figure to signal quality alone.

06What would make this more trustworthy

  • The stop-loss is lightly tested. Only 4 of 54 trades ever hit −20% — not much evidence for how it behaves in a genuinely novel tail event. Inverse-vol ETFs have a specific history of gapping through modeled stop levels overnight (Volmageddon again: an actual −80% single-day move, far beyond any daily stop's ability to fill at the modeled price).
  • No parameter sweep yet for this strategy (unlike the UVXY test) — the 25/10/−20/20d defaults haven't been stress-tested against nearby variants, so there's no read yet on how fragile the result is to small parameter changes.
  • Small sample. 54 trades over 14+ years, concentrated in a historically strong secular period for short volatility. A regime with sustained elevated vol (not just spikes) could look very different.

07Bottom line

This is a more credible strategy than the UVXY momentum idea: the win/loss sizing is balanced rather than upside-down, it rests on a real, documented phenomenon (the volatility risk premium), and its lagging entry structurally sidesteps the worst single-day gap risk that has historically destroyed short-vol trades. But roughly half the apparent edge shows up even from random timing in this same era, the −20% stop has essentially never been stress-tested by a true tail event, and 54 trades is not a large sample. Treat the headline 889% figure as "this era was very favorable to short vol, and this signal made it meaningfully better" rather than "this is a proven system" — and take the stop-loss discipline as seriously as the strategy's own history suggests it might one day be tested.
