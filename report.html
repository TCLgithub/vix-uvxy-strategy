VIX below 14: does a 3% UVXY scalp actually work?

Backtest and Monte Carlo verification of the discretionary rule: when VIX (normally 15–18) drops below 14, go long UVXY and take profit fast on a 3% pop. Tested on split-adjusted UVXY, 2011–2026.

02What was tested

  • Signal: VIX daily close below 14.00
  • Entry: next day's open in UVXY (no lookahead)
  • Exit, first to trigger: +3% take profit · −15% stop loss · 15 trading days max hold
  • Sizing: one position at a time, full capital redeployed per trade (a stress test, not a sizing recommendation)
  • Data: split-adjusted UVXY (handles its reverse splits) and ^VIX, both from UVXY's 2011-10-04 inception

04Why it loses: the payoff is upside-down

A 79.6% win rate looks good in isolation. It isn't, once you see the size of the wins versus the losses:

The average loss is 4.8× the average win. Exit breakdown across all 235 trades:

Take profit · 187 trades (79.6%) Stop loss · 45 trades (19.1%) Max hold · 3 trades (1.3%)
UVXY pops 3% often enough that the take-profit hits four times out of five. But when it goes the other way, it goes hard — almost 1 in 5 trades ran the full −15% stop. 187 wins × 3% (561 pts) doesn't cover 45 losses × 14.5% (653 pts).

Random-entry control (5,000 draws): same exit rule, but entry days are chosen at random instead of gated on VIX<14 — this isolates whether the signal itself carries edge, or whether the result is just UVXY's baseline volatility.

The signal shifts real weight out of the worst bin and into less-bad and positive outcomes — that's genuine edge. Real strategy beats the random control 85.5% of the time on total return, and 78.6% of the time on win rate. But both distributions are still dominated by losses: the control's median outcome is −95.2%, the signal's is −86.0%. Better than random isn't the same as good.

07Bottom line

The VIX<14 trigger is not noise — it times UVXY entries better than chance. But "wait for a 3% pop and take it" is the wrong way to harvest that edge: it caps the frequent, small wins while leaving the rare, large losses uncapped, which is exactly backwards for an instrument that decays hard and gaps violently. A materially different exit shape (wider target, tighter stop, short hold) can flip the expectancy positive in this sample, but the best version found still isn't an attractive risk/reward, and small parameter changes swing the result by 100+ percentage points — a sign of overfitting risk, not a robust edge. Before sizing this with real capital: either accept a much less asymmetric exit design and stress-test it further out-of-sample, or treat VIX<14 purely as a watch-list trigger for discretionary decisions rather than an automated scalp.
